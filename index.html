Evidence

The signal layer, measured — including where it fails

Most sentiment tooling returns a label with no way to check it. Ours returns numbers on a named psychological model (Russell's Circumplex), a second deterministic method as a control, and blind human ratings on the same 30 responses — scored by two independent raters.

Axis Model r 95% CI n Human ceiling Reading
Valence +0.96 [+0.92, +0.98] 30 +0.88 At the ceiling. The raters agree strongly, and the model matches what they can agree on.
Arousal +0.49 [+0.16, +0.72] 30 +0.71 Below the ceiling — this is model work, not ambiguity in the measure.
Hedging +0.81 [+0.64, +0.91] 30 +0.31 The raters agree only weakly on this axis, so the model's score is not claimed as validated. The rubric is being tightened and the axis re-rated.
Lexicon control +0.17 [-0.20, +0.50] 30 — A cheap deterministic word-lookup does not work here: not distinguishable from zero, and it contradicted the sign of the human mean on 14 of the 30 responses.

What the ceiling means. Two humans rated the same responses independently. How well they agree with each other is the ceiling: a model cannot be judged against an axis the raters themselves score differently. Valence 0.88 · arousal 0.71 · hedging 0.31.

What we are not claiming. n = 30 with two raters is an indicative measurement, not proof. A second pair of raters would tighten each ceiling. The LLM scorer is not fully deterministic, moving correlations by roughly 0.05 run to run. The lexicon control is licensed for research use and is not part of any shipped product.
