Signal extraction

Turning a single free-text response — in English, Hindi, or the code-mixed Hindi-English people actually speak — into three calibrated numbers: how pleasant or unpleasant the speaker feels, how activated they are, and how much they hedge. Built as a measurable instrument, not a sentiment score.

Most sentiment tooling returns a label and no way to check it. This returns numbers on a named psychological model, against a written schema, and reports its own accuracy — including where it is weak and where it fails outright.

What is measured

Valence and arousal are the two axes of Russell's Circumplex Model of Affect (Russell, 1980), scored independently — a calm, content response is pleasant with low arousal; fury is unpleasant with high arousal. Hedging is measured separately as qualifying language in the response itself.

How it works

Grounded, not vibesBoth axes are defined by a named, citable model before any scoring happens, so "how positive is this" is not the model's private judgment.
Structured output, forcedThe model must return the schema via a forced tool call. Nothing parses free text, so a malformed score cannot silently pass.
Code-mixed, nativelyHindi, English and Hinglish are scored as written. No translation step — translation is where tone is destroyed.

The ceiling is the number that makes each model r interpretable. A model cannot be judged against an axis the raters themselves score differently, and a model r that exceeds a low ceiling is not evidence of skill — it is evidence that the axis has no stable human answer to hit. With 2 raters there is a single pair, so this ceiling is itself a noisy estimate; three or four raters would tighten it.

What this does not prove yet

  • Two raters is the minimum for a ceiling, not the target. Valence now reads +0.96 against a ceiling of +0.88, which is a defensible number for this module. Every other axis needs more raters before its ceiling stops being one noisy pair.
  • Hedging has no usable human ground truth here. The raters agree at +0.31 — below the 0.50 threshold — so the model's +0.81 on that axis is not evidence it can measure hedging. It is evidence that "how much does this person hedge" is being read differently by different humans. Fixing the rubric is the next task, not tuning the model.
  • Arousal is the weak axis, and now that is measurable. +0.49 against a ceiling of +0.71: the model sits clearly below what the humans can agree on, so this is model error rather than ambiguity in the measure.
  • A model r above the ceiling is not proof. The model is correlated against the mean of the ratings, which is more reliable than either single rater, while the ceiling is computed between single raters. The two numbers are therefore not strictly comparable, and the ceiling is a floor on the axis's reliability, not a target the model has beaten.
  • The model is not deterministic. Two runs over the same 30 responses differed on roughly half the items (mean absolute difference 0.06 on valence, up to 0.50 on hedging), moving the reported correlation by about 0.05. Valence was stable; averaging repeated samples is the fix.
  • The lexicon cannot ship. NRC-VAD is licensed for research use; commercial use requires written consent from NRC, and redistribution is prohibited. It is used here as a research control only.
  • This validates a module, not a business. The claim above is about scoring one response. It says nothing yet about scale, cost per response, or behaviour on real deployment traffic.

Why it matters

Any team can call a model and print a sentiment score. The defensible asset is the discipline around it: a fixed schema, a citable psychological model, a second method as a control, a blind rating pack scored by more than one person, a measured result with its ceiling attached, and a written account of where it fails. That is what makes a capability claim auditable — by a client, a partner, or an investor's technical advisor. The same harness is what will train a domain-specific model later, because it already produces labelled data in a fixed format.
